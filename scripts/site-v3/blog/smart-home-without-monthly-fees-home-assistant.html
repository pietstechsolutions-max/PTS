A Smart Home Without Monthly Fees: Why We Build on Home Assistant

Five apps, three subscriptions and a doorbell that stops working when the internet does. There is a better way to run a smart home. Here is what Home Assistant is, why local control matters, and where we tell Long Island homeowners to start.

Published October 11, 2026 · 5 min read · by Piets Technology Solutions

Most smart homes grow one gadget at a time. A doorbell with its own app. Bulbs with another. A thermostat with a third, a garage opener with a fourth, and a couple of subscriptions to keep the good features turned on. It works, mostly — until the internet drops, a company changes its plan, or someone in the house just wants to turn the porch light on without opening four apps.

In short

  • Home Assistant is open-source software that runs your smart home on a small computer inside your house.
  • Local control means your automations keep working when the internet is down, with no required subscription.
  • Start with a few rooms that annoy you most, and get the home network right first.

That is why our smart home installs are built on Home Assistant.

What Home Assistant is

Home Assistant is free, open-source software that runs your smart home from a small computer that lives in your house — on a shelf, in the network closet, next to the router. Lights, locks, thermostats, shades, sensors and cameras from many different makers all show up in one app, and one set of automations ties them together: "when the last person leaves, lock the doors, drop the heat and turn off the lights."

Why local control matters

Many smart devices send every command out to a company's servers and back, even to turn on a light in the next room. Home Assistant keeps that conversation inside your house. That means:

  • It keeps working when the internet doesn't. Your automations, switches and schedules run during an outage.
  • Your data stays home. When you come and go and what your cameras see is not stored on someone else's server by default.
  • No required subscription. The software is free. You choose whether to pay for any optional cloud service.
  • Less risk when a company changes course. If a maker drops support for its app, a device that works locally keeps working.

One honest caveat: some devices only work through their maker's cloud. We check before anything is bought and steer you to devices that work locally.

The languages smart devices speak

You do not need to memorize these, but you will see them on the box:

  • Wi-Fi — familiar and easy, but dozens of small devices can crowd a home network.
  • Zigbee and Z-Wave — low-power mesh networks built for sensors, switches and locks. Each needs a small adapter connected to the Home Assistant computer.
  • Thread — a newer low-power mesh network for smart devices.
  • Matter — a shared smart-home standard, first published in 2022 and backed by the big tech companies, that runs over Wi-Fi, Ethernet or Thread and is designed for local control.

Home Assistant speaks all of them, so you can mix brands and keep what already works.

Where to start

Don't automate the whole house on day one. Start with what annoys you most:

  1. The front door. A smart lock, the porch light and the door camera working together: the light comes on with motion after dark, and you can see who is there and let them in from the same screen.
  2. Lighting scenes. "Dinner," "movie" and "goodnight" on a single button, with the wall switches still working for guests.
  3. Heating and cooling. Schedules that follow when people are actually home.
  4. Water leak sensors. A small sensor by the water heater, washing machine and sump pump that shuts a valve or sends an alert. Basements flood; this one pays attention.
  5. The garage door. A reminder when it has been left open after dark, and a button to close it from anywhere.

Cameras in the same app

Your camera system can live inside Home Assistant, so a doorbell press pops up the front-door view on your phone or the TV, and a motion event after midnight can turn on the outside lights. With an on-site recorder, such as the InVid Tech Paramont systems we install, the video stays home too — see security cameras.

Get the network right first

A smart home is only as reliable as the network under it. Weak Wi-Fi in the back bedroom means a lock that responds late or not at all. We check coverage first, wire the things that can be wired and put access points where the signal is weak — more on that in why one router isn't enough.

Keep it simple for everyone else in the house

The best smart home is one the rest of the family never has to think about. Light switches still switch lights. Locks still take a key or a code. Guests never need an app. Automation should remove steps, not add them.

Common questions

Do I need a monthly subscription for Home Assistant?

No. Home Assistant is free, open-source software that runs locally in your house. Any cloud add-on is optional.

Will my smart home still work if the internet goes down?

With Home Assistant and devices that support local control, yes. Your automations, switches and schedules keep running inside the house.

Can I keep the smart devices I already own?

Often, yes. Home Assistant works with devices from many makers. We check what you have first and tell you which ones work locally.

Will it work with my security cameras?

Yes. We integrate your camera system so the doorbell and door views show up in the same app as your lights and locks.
