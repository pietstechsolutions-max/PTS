Fuel comes to the truck. The truck keeps working.

A plug-in OBD-II device and an app that does the part nobody else joins up — it knows the vehicle is low, it knows where the vehicle is, and it sends the fuel company, the mechanic or the parts counter straight to it.

Concept console

This is the idea, running.

Pick a vehicle. Van 1 is sitting on a job at 18% fuel — send fuel to it instead of pulling it off the job. Then look at how service gets predicted and how an empty return leg gets filled.

Why build another one

Plenty of apps track a truck. None of them send anyone to it.

Tracking is solved. What is not solved is the bit after the alert — somebody still has to notice, phone around, and take the vehicle out of service to deal with it. That is the gap this fills.

It reads the vehicle

Mileage, fuel level, engine hours and fault codes come off the OBD-II port. Not typed in by a driver who forgot.

Fuel comes to you

Low tank plus a GPS pin equals a job for a fuel partner. They turn up at the site. The driver never leaves the work.

Service predicted

It learns how hard each vehicle gets used and flags the service before the noise starts, with the mileage and the code already attached for the shop.

Tap to pay in the field

The fuel driver or the technician takes payment on their own phone, tip included, settling to their own account. No terminal, no invoice to chase.

Footage that files itself

On an incident the clip locks, copies off the vehicle and goes to the insurer on file with the time, the track and the speed. Nobody has to remember three weeks later.

Thirty minutes out

The customer gets told automatically when the vehicle is half an hour away. The “what time are you coming” call stops.

Beyond work trucks

Anything that sits still with an empty tank.

Party buses & coaches

Parked outside a venue for four hours. Fuel it where it stands instead of the driver leaving the job.

Transport & haulage

The return legs get chained the way an airline chains flights, so the truck is not driving home empty.

Trades fleets

Plumbers, electricians, landscapers. Same problem, same fix, same partner network.

Ordinary drivers

Low and already late for work? Fuel delivered while the car is in the lot. Detailing while you are inside.

Where this honestly stands. Piets Fleet is in development, not shipping. The console above is a working demonstration of the concept, built from the real design — it is not reading a real vehicle. Nothing on this page is for sale yet, and no date is being promised. If you want in early, the next section is the one to read.

Two ways in

Fleets to pilot it. Partners to serve it.

The app is only worth anything if there is somebody on the other end of the alert. So it is being built with the fuel companies, shops and parts counters in from the start, not bolted on later.

If you run a fleet

Pilot vehicles get the device, the app and a say in what gets built next. Tell us the three things that waste the most time in your week.

  • Vehicle health, mileage and fuel without anybody writing it down
  • Service booked before it becomes a breakdown
  • Customers told automatically when the van is close
  • Incident footage handled without a scramble

If you are a potential partner

Fuel delivery, mobile mechanics, parts counters, detailers, insurance. The alerts the app raises become jobs, and they route to the partners in the network.

  • Work routed to you with the vehicle, the location and the fault already attached
  • Paid in the app, straight to your own account
  • Featured placement in the partner directory
  • Joint-venture conversations welcome — this is being built with partners, not at them
