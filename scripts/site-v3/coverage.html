The planner

Tap the plan. That is the whole interface.

Every camera you drop shades the part of the plan it is facing, in the shape of the lens you picked. Drag a camera to move it, drag its purple dot to turn it. The figures beside the plan are counted off your own layout — and each kind of space keeps its own.

Nothing leaves your device. The planner runs entirely in your browser. The only thing that ever reaches us is the summary line you choose to send with the form.

These are sample floor plans, not your building. They are here so you can show us the shape of what you want covered before anyone has measured anything.

What the number means, and what it does not. “Rough coverage” is the share of the sample plan that falls inside the shapes you drew — arithmetic on your own layout, nothing else. It is not a measurement, not an industry figure and not a quote. What a camera really shows you depends on mounting height, what is in the way, and what you need to be able to recognise.

Two tools, two jobs

The floor plan here, the building in the Design Studio.

This planner is for the shape of the place — which rooms, which doors, which corners matter. The Design Studio is for the building itself: load a photo of your own house or storefront and put the cameras on that.

  • Four sample spaces — house, storefront or restaurant, office, warehouse — and each one keeps its own layout
  • Three lens shapes, from a wide corner to a long run down a driveway
  • Cameras numbered CAM 1, CAM 2, the way the estimate will show them
  • Keyboard only, if you prefer — the plan itself takes focus and the arrow keys drive it

From a sketch to a real layout

What happens after you send it.

The sketch is the easy half. The layout that gets installed comes from looking at the property properly — and most of that can happen before anyone drives out.

  • You send the summary the planner writes for you, with the address
  • We look at the property on the public record — the footprint, the approaches, where cable can run
  • A real layout comes back, with the approximate view from each position
  • One walk-through settles mounting height and the last few inches, then the number is the number
