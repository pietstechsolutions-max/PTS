In most cases, you do not need a permit for security cameras in Suffolk County. A typical home or small-business camera system is low-voltage work: the cameras run on a network cable back to a recorder, and nothing touches your electrical panel. That's why most installs in Huntington, Smithtown, Brookhaven, Islip, and Babylon go in without any paperwork. There are a few exceptions, and a few privacy rules that matter more than the permit question. Here's the plain-English version.

## When you don't need a permit for security cameras in Suffolk County

The everyday install: four to eight cameras mounted under the eaves or soffits, cables run through the attic or basement, and a recorder sitting near your router. This is low-voltage cabling, the same category as your internet or cable TV wiring. Towns in Suffolk County generally do not require a building or electrical permit for it.

That covers:

- Wired IP cameras powered over the network cable (PoE)
- Wi-Fi cameras that plug into an existing outlet
- Video doorbells that use existing doorbell wiring
- The recorder, the app setup, and your phone alerts

The InVid Tech Paramont systems we install are PoE, so there's one cable per camera and no new electrical work. That keeps the job simple and keeps you out of the permit office.

## When a permit or licensed trade might come up

There are a handful of situations where the answer changes:

- **New electrical outlets.** If a camera location needs a new 120-volt outlet, that part is electrical work. In Suffolk County that calls for a licensed electrician, and your town may want a permit for it.
- **Exterior conduit or trenching.** Running cable underground to a detached garage, a gate, or a barn can involve digging. Call 811 before any dig, and check with your town if you're crossing a property line or a public right-of-way.
- **Poles and structures.** A camera on a new pole or a tall mast in the yard may count as a structure in some towns, especially in villages with tighter zoning.
- **Historic districts and villages.** Some incorporated villages have architectural review rules for anything visible from the street. Rare for cameras, but worth a quick call.
- **Commercial buildings.** Retail, restaurants, and offices sometimes fall under fire and building code reviews that homes don't.

The rule of thumb: if the job is just cameras and network cable, you're fine. If it adds power, digging, or a structure, ask your town building department first. Every town and village in Suffolk has its own rules, so a two-minute phone call beats a guess.

## Privacy rules that matter more than permits

The question people should be asking is not "do I need a permit" but "where can I point these things." A few guidelines we follow on every install across [Long Island](/locations/long-island.html):

- **Your property, your driveway, the street in front.** All normal. Public spaces and your own land are fair game.
- **Not into a neighbor's windows, yard, or pool.** Aim cameras to cover your doors, driveway, and approaches. If a neighbor's space ends up in the frame, most systems let us black out that area with a privacy mask.
- **Audio is the tricky part.** New York is a one-party consent state for recording conversations, but recording audio of people who aren't talking to you can create problems. Many owners simply turn audio off on exterior cameras.
- **Tenants and employees.** Cameras in private spaces like bathrooms and changing areas are off-limits. Common areas and entrances are generally fine. A "video surveillance in use" sign is a simple, smart move.

None of this is legal advice. It's what has kept our customers on good terms with the people next door.

## FAQ

### Do I need a permit for security cameras in Suffolk County if I own the house?
Usually not. A standard camera system with network cabling is low-voltage work and does not require a building permit in most Suffolk County towns. Permits only tend to come up if the job adds a new electrical outlet, underground trenching, or a new pole or structure.

### Can I install cameras on a rental property on Long Island?
Yes, with limits. Exterior cameras covering entrances, driveways, and common areas are normal. Cameras inside a tenant's private living space are not. Tell tenants in writing where the cameras are.

### Is it legal to record audio on my cameras in New York?
New York allows recording a conversation you're part of, but recording strangers' conversations can be a problem. Most homeowners turn audio off on outdoor cameras and keep video only.
