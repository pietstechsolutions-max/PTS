Most local business websites are brochures. They have a logo, a list of services, a stock photo of a handshake and a contact form that nobody fills out. They look fine. They bring in almost nothing.

If you run a plumbing, landscaping, auto, cleaning or remodeling business on Long Island, your website has one job: **turn a stranger on their phone into a call, a text or a quote request.** Everything else is decoration.

## What the people visiting your site actually want

Picture who is on your site. Someone with water on the floor. Someone whose lawn guy stopped showing up. Someone who just got rear-ended. They are on a phone, they are stressed, and they will give you about five seconds.

They want three answers, fast:

1. **Do you do what I need?**
2. **Do you come to my town?**
3. **How do I reach you right now?**

If your site makes them dig for any of those, they hit back and call the next name on Google.

## The six things a contractor website needs

**1. A tap-to-call button that follows them down the page.** On a phone, the call button should always be one thumb away.

**2. "What do you need?" buttons.** Leak, clogged drain, weekly mowing, accident repair. One tap starts a quote with that job already picked.

**3. A service area they can check.** A list of the towns you serve and a box where they can type their own.

**4. Real photos and real reviews.** Your trucks, your crew, your finished jobs. Never fake reviews. Customers can smell them, and Google can too.

**5. A quote form that lands on your phone.** Not in an inbox you check on Sunday. A text the moment it comes in, and an automatic text back to the customer so the lead doesn't go cold while you're on a ladder.

**6. Speed.** If it takes more than a couple of seconds to load on a phone, half of them are gone.

## Where most sites lose the job

The lead comes in at 4:30 on a Tuesday while you're under a sink. You see it at 8 that night. By then they hired someone else. That's not a website problem. That's a follow-up problem.
