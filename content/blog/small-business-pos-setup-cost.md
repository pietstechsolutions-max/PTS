"Free POS" is one of the most expensive phrases in small business. Someone will happily hand you a tablet and a card reader at no charge — and make it back, with interest, on every swipe for the next three years. Here's what a point-of-sale setup really costs for a deli, restaurant, café, salon or shop, and the questions that separate a good deal from a trap.

## The four buckets

Every POS quote, no matter who it's from, breaks down into the same four buckets:

### 1. Hardware (one-time)

- **Terminals / registers.** A counter station with a customer-facing screen, or an iPad on a stand, or both.
- **Payment readers.** Tap, chip and swipe. One per register, plus handhelds for tableside or curbside.
- **Printers.** Receipt printers at the counter, kitchen printers or a kitchen display screen (KDS) in the back.
- **Cash drawers, scanners, scales** depending on the business.
- **Network gear.** The part everyone forgets — see bucket 4.

A single-register café is at the low end; a full-service restaurant with three stations, two kitchen printers, a KDS and four handhelds is at the high end. Hardware is the most visible cost but usually not the biggest over time.

### 2. Software (monthly)

Most modern systems charge per month, per location, sometimes per register. Add-ons like online ordering, loyalty, gift cards, reservations and payroll each carry their own fee. Read the add-on list before you sign — the base price is rarely what you'll actually pay.

### 3. Payment processing (per transaction)

This is the bucket that matters most, and the one that's hardest to compare. Two common structures:

- **Flat rate** — something like a fixed percentage plus a few cents per transaction. Simple, predictable, usually more expensive at higher volume.
- **Interchange-plus** — the actual card network cost plus a fixed markup. Cheaper for most businesses doing more than a modest monthly volume, but the statement is harder to read.

A restaurant doing solid volume can easily pay more in processing in a year than the entire hardware install cost. Do that math before you get excited about free terminals.

### 4. Installation and network (one-time, then support)

Your POS is only as reliable as the network under it. That means:

- A **wired connection** to every fixed register and kitchen printer (Wi-Fi is for handhelds, not the main station)
- A **dedicated network** for POS, separate from guest Wi-Fi
- **Backup internet** (a cellular failover) so a cable outage doesn't stop you from taking cards
- Clean **cable runs** to the kitchen, the bar and the host stand
- **Menu boards** and **cameras** on the same clean network if you're doing a full build-out

This is where a low-voltage installer earns their keep. A POS company will ship you boxes. Someone has to run the cables, mount the screens, configure the network and make it all work during a Friday rush.

## The questions to ask before you sign

1. **What is the total monthly cost with every feature I'll actually use?** Not the base price.
2. **What is my effective processing rate?** Ask for an estimate based on your real monthly volume and average ticket.
3. **Is there a contract, and what does it cost to leave?** "Free" hardware often comes with a multi-year term and early-termination fees.
4. **Do I own the hardware?** If the answer is no, the free terminal is a lease.
5. **What happens when the internet goes down?** Offline mode, and for how long?
6. **Who installs it and who do I call at 9 pm on Saturday?** The POS vendor's support line, or a local person who can actually show up?
